Lovable: When Building Becomes Cheap

A Stockholm start-up turned 'describe your app in a sentence' into one of Europe's fastest-growing software companies.

LovableSweden2023–2026

Key Facts

  • Lovable was started in Stockholm in 2023 by Anton Osika and Fabian Hedin; its AI app builder, which turns plain-language descriptions into websites and apps, opened to the public in November 2024 (Riley, 2026; Heim, 2025).
  • In July 2025, about eight months after launch, Lovable raised a $200 million Series A led by Accel at a $1.8 billion valuation, with 45 full-time employees (Heim, 2025).
  • At that point it reported $75 million in annual recurring revenue, more than 180,000 paying subscribers, over 2.3 million active users and 10 million projects created (Heim, 2025).
  • In December 2025 Lovable raised $330 million at a $6.6 billion valuation, led by CapitalG and Menlo Ventures; it had reached $100 million ARR within eight months of launch and $200 million four months later (Szkutak, 2025).
  • In March 2025 a security researcher found that 170 of 1,645 analysed Lovable-built apps had missing or weak database access rules, exposing data such as emails and API keys; the flaw was published as CVE-2025-48757 in May 2025 (Palmer, 2025).
  • In August 2026 Lovable raised $400 million at a $13.3 billion valuation, led by Menlo Ventures and the Scaleup Europe Fund, reporting $500 million ARR in June 2026 and more than 60 million projects built (Riley, 2026).

The Storyline (after Bostelaar)

Beginning · Misbelief

Most people assume that the hard part of starting a digital business is building the software, so a founder without a developer is stuck. Business plans used to spend pages on the technical build and its cost.

Middle · Insight

Lovable turned building into a conversation: users describe the app and the AI writes the code, so a first version takes hours, not months. But the security findings of 2025 showed that a fast build is not a safe or validated business; speed moves the risk from 'can we build it?' to 'should we, and will anyone pay?'.

End · Understanding

When building is cheap, the entrepreneur's real work is choosing the right problem, testing assumptions with real users and designing a viable business model. The business plan becomes a set of hypotheses that the MVP is built to test.

Your Task

In your hackathon team, act as advisers to a non-technical founder who wants to launch a Munich student-housing app built with an AI app builder. Use the Business Model Canvas to map Lovable's own business model and identify which block creates its advantage. Then use Testing Business Ideas — Desirability, Feasibility, Viability to rank the founder's three riskiest assumptions, including one about data security. Finish with a one-page Build–Measure–Learn plan for the first two weeks.

Discussion Questions

  1. Analyse: Which of Lovable's nine BMC blocks explain its speed of growth, and which blocks are most exposed to competition from other AI app builders?
  2. Evaluate: Lovable-built apps exposed user data because founders shipped fast without proper access rules. How far is the platform responsible, and how far the founder who launched the app?
  3. Create: Design a two-week test plan for a new venture built on an AI app builder that checks desirability, feasibility (including security) and viability before any money is spent on marketing.
