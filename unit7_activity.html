BDIBM313 · Unit 7 · Debate

Debate — Born or Made? The Oxford Motion

Two benches argue an Oxford-style motion with evidence from Jumia and the unit frameworks; the floor votes before and after.

Sociological Considerations for Entrepreneurship in Digitalization · Format: Debate

Learning Focus · ILOs 2 & 3

Critically assess how culture, institutions, role models and support systems create the social conditions for digital entrepreneurship, and reflect on your own cognitive adaptability as a future founder.

Founders are made by context: culture, role models and support systems shape who dares to start.

Warm-up · 10 min · The Human Spectrum Line

Tape a line across the room: one end is 'Founders are born', the other 'Founders are made'. Every student stands where they honestly believe they belong and tells a neighbour one person (a parent, a teacher, a creator) who shaped that view. The lecturer photographs the line: this is the pre-debate vote.

The Provocation

Jumia was called 'the Amazon of Africa'. Then it shut down food delivery in seven countries.

The same app, the same code, the same founders — but Lagos is not Berlin.

Your passport, your parents and your postcode may predict your start-up more than your personality does.

If founders are born, why do some countries produce so many more of them?

Tonight you will argue a side you might not believe. That is the point.

Read the lines slowly, one per slide, straight after the spectrum line. Leave a silent pause after line three and ask two students at opposite ends of the line to react in one sentence each before you reveal the motion.

How the Activity Runs

1
Case briefing: Jumia's reset20 min

In groups of four, students read the one-page Jumia fact sheet and mark every fact as 'context' (culture, institutions, infrastructure) or 'founder choice' (leadership, strategy). Each group shares its single most surprising fact. Output: a sticky-note wall split into Context and Choice.

2
Draw sides and build the case25 min

Teams draw a card: Proposition ('made, not born'), Opposition ('born, not made') or Floor Judges. Proposition and Opposition prepare three arguments, each backed by one Jumia fact and one named framework (e.g. Institutional Pillars, Cognitive Adaptability, Entrepreneurial Self-Efficacy). Judges build a scoring sheet: evidence, framework use, rebuttal, delivery.

3
Oxford-style debate40 min

Four speakers per bench alternate (Proposition first), four minutes each, with points of information allowed after the first and before the last minute. No laptops or phones at the lectern: speakers may use one index card only. The lecturer chairs and keeps time with a bell.

4
Floor debate and division vote15 min

The floor asks questions and makes 60-second interventions. Students then physically 'divide' by walking to the Proposition or Opposition side of the room. The bench that moved more votes compared with the warm-up spectrum line wins; the judges announce the best speaker with reasons.

5
Framework clinic20 min

Mixed trios (one from each bench plus a judge) place Jumia on two frameworks drawn on A3: Scott's Institutional Pillars and Hersey and Blanchard's Situational Leadership. They agree one recommendation for a founder launching a digital venture in a market they do not know. Output: an annotated A3 sheet photographed for the course portal.

Deliverable. Each bench submits a one-page argument map (claim, Jumia evidence, framework, rebuttal) and each trio uploads a photo of its annotated A3 framework sheet with one recommendation.
