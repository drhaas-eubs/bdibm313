Jumia: Resetting the 'Amazon of Africa'

How a pan-African e-commerce pioneer cut back to fit its context — and what that says about founders and their environment.

Jumia Technologies AGPan-African (Nigeria, Egypt, Kenya, Morocco and others); German holding, Nasdaq-listed2022–2026

Key Facts

  • In November 2022 co-founders Jeremy Hodara and Sacha Poignonnec stepped down as co-CEOs; Francis Dufay became acting CEO and was later confirmed in the role (Kene-Okafor, 2023).
  • In Q4 2022 Jumia cut more than 900 roles, about 20% of its workforce across 11 markets, reduced managerial roles in Dubai by 60% and discontinued Jumia Prime in all markets (Kene-Okafor, 2023).
  • In December 2023 Jumia announced it would close its food delivery business, operating since 2013, in seven countries — Nigeria, Kenya, Morocco, Côte d'Ivoire, Tunisia, Uganda and Algeria — by the end of 2023 (TechCabal, 2023).
  • For full-year 2023 Jumia reported an adjusted EBITDA loss of $58.2 million, down 68% year on year, while sales and advertising expenses fell 63% and revenue declined 8% to $186 million (Kene-Okafor, 2024).
  • In 2024 Jumia ceased operations in South Africa and Tunisia, which had contributed only 3.5% and 2.7% of orders, and announced a push 'upcountry' beyond major cities (Akintaro, 2024).
  • In February 2026 Jumia exited Algeria, leaving eight markets, reported FY2025 revenue of $188.9 million (up 13%) and targeted full-year profitability in 2027 (Trendtype, 2026).

The Storyline (after Bostelaar)

Beginning · Misbelief

Early investors and media pitched Jumia as 'the Amazon of Africa': copy a proven digital model, add capital, expand into many countries fast, and scale will follow. Founder vision and money were treated as the decisive forces.

Middle · Insight

The context fought back. Fragmented logistics, low card payment use, varied regulation and thin margins in categories such as food delivery meant that the same model performed very differently from country to country. The new CEO cut markets, staff and marketing, relocated management closer to customers and focused on physical goods.

End · Understanding

Digital ventures are socio-technical systems: technology scales easily, but institutions, culture and infrastructure do not. Jumia's reset shows that adaptive founders read their context, admit what does not fit, and redesign the business around local realities rather than imported templates.

Your Task

In your debate bench or clinic trio, use Scott's Institutional Pillars to sort the forces behind Jumia's reset into regulative, normative and cultural-cognitive pressures. Then use Cognitive Adaptability to diagnose how the leadership team changed its thinking between 2022 and 2026: which assumptions did it drop, and what evidence made it change? Finally, apply Situational Leadership to explain why a new CEO style was needed after the founders left. Present your answer as an annotated A3 sheet with one recommendation for a founder entering an unfamiliar market.

Discussion Questions

  1. Analyse: Which of Jumia's problems were caused by its context (institutions, infrastructure, culture) and which by its own strategic choices?
  2. Evaluate: Was closing food delivery and leaving South Africa, Tunisia and Algeria a sign of failure or of entrepreneurial adaptability? Justify with evidence.
  3. Create: Design a 'context check' of five questions that a Munich-based digital start-up should answer before expanding into a market such as Nigeria or Kenya.
