Roundtable — The Pattern Hunters

Present your key case findings, rehearse your Digital Venture Project, then find the patterns that connect ten real ventures.

Key Case Findings and Roundtable Discussion · Format: Roundtable · Capstone

Learning Focus · Integrates ILOs 1–4

Integrate the course by comparing ten real digital ventures, critically assessing the technical and social conditions behind their outcomes, reflecting on your own entrepreneurial leadership, and communicating how digitally governed businesses differ from traditional ones.

The cases differ, the lesson repeats: digital advantage belongs to founders who test, reflect and adapt faster than their context changes.

Warm-up · 10 min · One Case, One Word

Ten case cards (Gorillas to Sama/OpenAI) are laid out on a table at the front. Each student walks up, places one sticky note with a single word on the case that taught them the most, and says the word aloud in under ten seconds. The lecturer reads the clusters back to the room: these words are the raw material for today’s roundtable.

The Provocation

Ten ventures. Five countries of origin at least. One question: what repeats?

Gorillas grew faster than its unit economics; Builder.ai grew faster than its truth.

Vinted waited thirteen years for its first annual profit; Lovable raised $200 million eight months after launch.

If every case has a different ending, why do the same mistakes keep reappearing?

Today you stop collecting stories and start finding patterns.

Read the lines slowly, then leave the last line on screen during the presentations. Ask the class to note any pattern they hear repeated across the group presentations — they will need it in the roundtable.

How the Activity Runs

1
Key-findings presentations35 min

Each group (4–5 students) has five minutes to present its key case findings and the core idea of its Digital Venture Project, structured with the Pyramid Principle: answer first, three supporting arguments, evidence underneath. No more than three slides or one flip chart. This is the dress rehearsal for the final project due on 21 January 2027.

2
Six-Hats peer feedback15 min

After each presentation, about two minutes of rapid feedback follow. Before the session, every other group is assigned one coloured hat (white = facts, black = risks, yellow = value, green = ideas, red = gut feeling, blue = process) and gives exactly one comment from that perspective on a feedback card handed to the presenters.

3
Cross-case matrix25 min

Groups receive an A1 matrix with the ten cases as rows and five columns: digital lever, enabling condition, main risk, how the founders learned, outcome. Each group fills two assigned rows from the Roundtable Dossier, then walks the room to read the others and circles any pattern that appears in at least three cases.

4
Moderated roundtable30 min

Chairs form one large circle with an inner ring of six speaking seats. Each group sends one delegate with a Toulmin claim card (claim, data, warrant, qualifier, rebuttal) about one cross-case pattern; other students may tap in to take a seat. The lecturer moderates with the blue hat and logs each pattern on the board.

5
After-Action Review and Johari check15 min

Groups answer the four After-Action Review questions about their project rehearsal on one A4 sheet. Then each student writes one Johari blind spot that the peer feedback revealed and one action to close it before submission.

Deliverable. Each group submits a photo of its completed cross-case matrix rows, one Toulmin claim card, and its After-Action Review sheet; each student keeps the peer-feedback cards for revising the Digital Venture Project.
