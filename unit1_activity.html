BDIBM313 · Unit 1 · Presentation

Presentation — The Thumbnail Pitch

Teams redesign a real traditional Munich business as a digital venture on one hand-drawn sheet and pitch it live in three minutes.

Introduction to Entrepreneurship in Digitalization · Format: Presentation

Learning Focus · ILOs 1 & 4

Investigate how digital artifacts, platforms and infrastructure change the way a venture creates and delivers value, and communicate the main differences between a traditional and a digitally governed business.

Going digital does not change what customers need; it changes who can serve them, how fast and at what cost.

Warm-up · 10 min · Digital or Not?

The lecturer shows ten logos one after another (for example N26, a local bakery, Flix, Vinted, a Munich driving school, Too Good To Go, Deutsche Bahn, Celonis, a hair salon, Zalando). Students stand up if they think the business is a digital venture, stay seated if it is traditional, and raise one hand if it is a hybrid. After each logo one volunteer explains their choice in one sentence.

The Provocation

Your customers still want groceries, a bank account and a taxi. That has not changed since 1990.

What changed is who can deliver them, how fast and at what cost.

Gorillas became a unicorn in about ten months and disappeared in four years.

Speed is a digital superpower. It is also the fastest way to burn money.

Today you decide which part of a business really becomes digital, and which part stays a bicycle in the rain.

Read the lines slowly with a photo of a Gorillas rider on screen. Then ask the class: 'Was Gorillas a digital company or a delivery company with an app?' Write the two camps on the board and return to them after the case clinic.

How the Activity Runs

1
Case clinic: dissect Gorillas25 min

In groups of four, students read the Gorillas fact sheet and sort every element of the business into three columns: digital artifact (app, data), digital platform (matching of orders, riders and stock) and physical operation (dark stores, bikes, riders). Each group decides where the money was lost and writes one sentence on a flipchart.

2
Pick a real traditional business10 min

Each group draws one card with a real, traditional business type found within 1 km of campus (bakery, florist, driving school, dry cleaner, independent bookshop, fitness studio). They agree on one concrete example they can visit or call later for Assessment 1.

3
Draw the Thumbnail20 min

On one A3 sheet, by hand and without laptops, the group draws a single picture that shows the redesigned business: what stays physical, what becomes a digital artifact, which platform or infrastructure it uses, and one headline of no more than twelve words. They place it on the Degrees of Digitization scale (mild, moderate, extreme).

4
The Thumbnail Pitch35 min

Each group presents its sheet in exactly three minutes, followed by two minutes of questions from a randomly chosen 'investor' group. Every presenter must name one risk the digital model creates that the traditional model did not have.

5
Peer scoring and debrief20 min

Students score each pitch with dot stickers on clarity, realism and customer value. The lecturer links the winning pitches to Nambisan's three elements, the Westerman matrix and the core message, and students begin their written reflection.

Deliverable. One hand-drawn A3 thumbnail per group, photographed and uploaded to Moodle, plus a three-minute live pitch that names the customer need, the digital change and one new risk.
