BDIBM313 · Unit 1 · Reflection Prompt

Reflection & Your Personal Slibrary

Key takeaways and three questions for independent study

Introduction to Entrepreneurship in Digitalization · Format: Presentation

Learning Focus · ILOs 1 & 4

Investigate how digital artifacts, platforms and infrastructure change the way a venture creates and delivers value, and communicate the main differences between a traditional and a digitally governed business.

Key Takeaways

  • Customer needs stay remarkably stable; digital technology changes who can serve them, how fast and at what cost.
  • A venture is digital when software-based artifacts and platforms are its core, not when it simply has an app or a website.
  • Digital infrastructure such as cloud services and online payments has made starting a venture cheaper, but also made copying a venture easier.
  • Hybrid ventures must manage two kinds of economics: near-zero-cost software and physical costs that grow with every order.
  • Digital transformation is a question of strategy and leadership, not only of buying technology.

Individually. In writing. No discussion.

Three Questions for Independent Study

  1. Gorillas could raise money, open cities and win customers at digital speed, but each delivery still cost real money. Which single decision in the Gorillas story would you have made differently, and why?
  2. Think about your role in today’s Thumbnail Pitch. Did you lead, support, challenge or stay quiet? What does that tell you about how you behave when a group must decide fast under time pressure, and what will you try differently next week?
  3. Choose one framework from today’s gallery (for example Nambisan's Digital Entrepreneurship Elements or the Westerman Digital Maturity Matrix) and explain it in your own words in 150–200 words for your personal Slibrary, using one example from Munich.

Question three becomes your independent-study task and the next entry in your personal Slibrary. Cite your sources in Harvard style.
