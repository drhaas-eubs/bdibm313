BDIBM313 · Unit 3 · Reflection Prompt

Reflection & Your Personal Slibrary

Key takeaways and three questions for independent study

Digital Entrepreneurial Processes · Format: Mini-Hackathon

Learning Focus · ILOs 1 & 2

Investigate how AI and no-code tools change the way a digital venture moves from idea to tested business model, and critically assess the technical and market conditions that make a fast launch possible.

Key Takeaways

  • A business plan for a digital venture is a set of hypotheses that must be tested with real customers.
  • The Business Model Canvas puts the whole business model on one page so a team can see, test and change it quickly.
  • AI and no-code tools make MVPs cheap and fast, which shifts the founder's challenge from building to choosing and validating.
  • Fast building creates new risks, such as weak data security, that must be tested as part of feasibility.
  • Progress in a start-up is measured by validated learning, not by the number of features shipped.

Individually. In writing. No discussion.

Three Questions for Independent Study

  1. Lovable lets almost anyone build an app. Looking at the case, what do you think will separate the founders who succeed with such tools from those who fail, and why?
  2. During today's hackathon, which role did you naturally take (builder, tester, presenter, organiser) and how did you react when the test showed that one of your assumptions was wrong? What would you do differently as a team leader next time?
  3. Choose one framework from today's Slibraries and explain it in your own words in 150–200 words for your personal Slibrary, including one example from your own venture idea.

Question three becomes your independent-study task and the next entry in your personal Slibrary. Cite your sources in Harvard style.
