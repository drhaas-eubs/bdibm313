Sama & OpenAI: The Human Cost of a Cleaner Chatbot

A TIME investigation into Kenyan data labellers raised the question of who is responsible for the hidden work behind AI.

Sama (Samasource) and OpenAIKenya / United States2021–2026

Key Facts

  • TIME reported on 18 January 2023 that, from November 2021, OpenAI contracted Sama to have workers in Nairobi label text describing sexual abuse, hate speech and violence to help build a safety filter for ChatGPT (Perrigo, 2023).
  • According to TIME, workers took home between about $1.32 and $2 per hour depending on seniority and performance, while documents showed OpenAI paid Sama an hourly rate of $12.50; the contracts were worth about $200,000 in total (Perrigo, 2023).
  • TIME reported that Sama ended the work in February 2022, eight months earlier than planned; Sama said employees had access to individual and group sessions with licensed mental health therapists, while workers interviewed described the sessions as rare and unhelpful (Perrigo, 2023).
  • In January 2023 Sama announced it would close its content-moderation business in Nairobi, cutting about 200 jobs (3% of its workforce) to focus on computer-vision data annotation (Njanja, 2023a).
  • In July 2023 former workers petitioned Kenya's parliament to investigate the outsourcing of harmful content work; Sama said it welcomed third-party audits and paid fair wages, and OpenAI described the work as challenging and valuable to AI safety (Njanja, 2023b).
  • The Guardian reported in August 2023 that 51 moderators had worked on the OpenAI project and that, according to the petitioners, they earned between $1.46 and $3.74 per hour; OpenAI declined to comment and Sama said therapists were available 24/7 (Rowe, 2023).
  • In April 2026 the Associated Press reported that Sama would lay off 1,108 employees in Nairobi after Meta ended a major engagement; separate lawsuits by former Meta moderators against Sama and Meta, which both companies contest, were still ongoing (Associated Press, 2026).

The Storyline (after Bostelaar)

Beginning · Misbelief

Many people believed AI systems like ChatGPT become safe automatically. Outsourcing seemed a neutral business choice: the client pays a contractor, the contractor employs workers under local law, and everyone's responsibility ends at the contract.

Middle · Insight

Investigative reporting made visible the human labour behind AI safety and the gap between what the client paid and what workers reportedly took home. Companies and workers gave very different accounts of the support offered, showing that 'legal and paid above local minimum' does not settle the ethical question.

End · Understanding

Responsibility in digital supply chains is shared. Founders who outsource knowledge work must ask in advance what fair pay, safe conditions and real voice mean for the people doing it — not only after a journalist asks.

Your Task

Act as an ethics advisory board for an AI start-up that plans to outsource data labelling. First, analyse the Sama/OpenAI case with Carroll's CSR Pyramid: on which layer did the reported problems arise? Second, score the reported working conditions against the Fairwork Principles for platform work, clearly separating reported facts from company statements. Third, use the Three Ethical Theories to decide whether the start-up should outsource, insource or redesign the task. Present a one-page recommendation.

Discussion Questions

  1. Analyse: Which parties — client, contractor, workers, investors, regulators — had influence over working conditions, and how did their incentives differ?
  2. Evaluate: Is paying above the local minimum wage sufficient to meet ethical duties for traumatic digital work? Judge with at least two frameworks from this unit.
  3. Create: Draft three clauses for an outsourcing contract that would protect data workers while keeping the service viable for a start-up.
