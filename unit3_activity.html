BDIBM313 · Unit 3 · Mini-Hackathon

Mini-Hackathon — 90 Minutes to a Clickable MVP

Teams build a clickable MVP with free no-code and AI tools, test it on real classmates and map it on a Business Model Canvas.

Digital Entrepreneurial Processes · Format: Mini-Hackathon

Learning Focus · ILOs 1 & 2

Investigate how AI and no-code tools change the way a digital venture moves from idea to tested business model, and critically assess the technical and market conditions that make a fast launch possible.

A business plan is a hypothesis to test, not a promise to keep.

Warm-up · 10 min · The Napkin Problem

Each student writes one everyday annoyance from student life in Munich on a sticky note (housing, deadlines, transport, food, part-time jobs) and sticks it on the wall. Everyone then gets three dot stickers to vote for the problems they would pay to have solved. The five most-voted problems become the hackathon challenge list.

The Provocation

In 2024 you needed a developer to build an app. In 2026 you need a sentence.

If anyone can build the product in an afternoon, the product is no longer your advantage.

Your business plan is a list of guesses. Today you will find out which ones are wrong.

Nobody will remember how pretty your prototype was. They will remember whether a stranger used it.

Read the lines slowly with the Lovable growth figures on screen, then ask: 'So what is left for the entrepreneur to do?' Collect three answers on the board and return to them in the debrief.

How the Activity Runs

1
Frame the problem and the customer20 min

Teams of four pick one problem from the wall and write a one-line value proposition ('For [customer] who [problem], our [product] [benefit]'). They list their three riskiest assumptions on cards and label each as desirability, feasibility or viability. Output: a problem card and three assumption cards.

2
Build sprint with no-code and AI tools40 min

Using the free tier of an AI app builder such as Lovable, or a no-code tool such as Glide or a Figma clickable prototype, each team builds the smallest version that lets a user complete one core task. One member acts as product owner and keeps a prompt log of what the AI got right and wrong. No real personal data may be collected; use dummy data only.

3
Guerrilla user test20 min

Teams swap laptops with another team: two 'users' try the MVP while the hosts stay silent and observe. Hosts record where users hesitate, what they say aloud, and whether they would sign up (yes/maybe/no). Output: a test card with at least five observations and one metric.

4
Map and iterate on the Business Model Canvas20 min

On a printed A1 Business Model Canvas, teams map their venture with sticky notes in two colours: green for what the test supported and orange for what is still an untested guess. They decide whether to persevere or pivot one block and write the next experiment they would run.

5
Demo and debrief20 min

Each team gives a 2-minute live demo (no slides) and names one assumption the test killed. The lecturer closes by linking the results to the Build–Measure–Learn loop and the Digital Venture Project (Assessment 2).

Deliverable. A working clickable MVP link or screen recording, a photographed BMC with green and orange sticky notes, and a test card showing one assumption validated or invalidated.
