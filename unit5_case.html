Langfuse: Pivoting Inside the Accelerator

How three Berlin founders found their real idea in the middle of Y Combinator.

LangfuseGermany2022–2026

Key Facts

  • Langfuse was founded in Berlin in 2022 by Marc Klingen, Max Deichmann and Clemens Rawert and joined Y Combinator's Winter 2023 batch (Y Combinator, 2026).
  • The founders were accepted into YC in November 2022 with Finto, a usage-based billing product, and pivoted roughly three weeks into the programme (Langfuse, 2026; Black, 2024).
  • During the batch they tested several AI application ideas, typically spending about two weeks on each, before deciding to build open-source tooling for teams building LLM applications (Langfuse, 2026).
  • Langfuse launched publicly in summer 2023 and became Product Hunt's Product of the Day (Langfuse, 2023).
  • In November 2023 it announced a USD 4 million seed round from Lightspeed Venture Partners, La Famiglia and Y Combinator (Langfuse, 2023).
  • The project passed 10,000 GitHub stars in April 2025 and open-sourced all product features under the MIT licence in June 2025 (Langfuse, 2026).
  • On 16 January 2026 ClickHouse acquired Langfuse while announcing a USD 400 million Series D at a USD 15 billion valuation led by Dragoneer (Temkin, 2026).
  • At the time of the deal Langfuse reported more than 2,000 paying customers and over 26 million monthly SDK installs (Orrick, 2026).

The Storyline (after Bostelaar)

Beginning · Misbelief

Most students think founders enter an accelerator with a finished idea and leave with money. Langfuse arrived with a billing product that customers liked but the founders did not believe in, in a mature market.

Middle · Insight

The real value of the batch was proximity: dozens of other teams were building AI applications at the same moment and all struggled to see what their LLM apps were actually doing. By rapidly testing ideas and listening to batch-mates, the founders found a painful, shared problem and turned it into an open-source product.

End · Understanding

An ecosystem shortens the distance between a founder and the people who reveal the opportunity, test it and fund it. Langfuse's open-source strategy then turned those early users into a community, which became more defensible than any patent and ultimately attracted an acquirer.

Your Task

In your group, map Langfuse's stakeholders at three moments (November 2022, November 2023, January 2026) on a Power–Interest Grid and mark which became 'definitive' stakeholders using Stakeholder Salience. Then use Teece's Profiting from Innovation to explain why Langfuse chose an open-source licence rather than patents to capture value. Finally, apply the Incubators vs Accelerators distinction to decide whether your own venture idea needs an incubator, an accelerator or neither right now. Present your answer on one A3 sheet.

Discussion Questions

  1. Analyse: Which three stakeholders had the greatest influence on Langfuse's pivot, and what kind of power did each hold?
  2. Evaluate: Was giving equity to Y Combinator worth it for Langfuse, given that the original idea was abandoned? Justify with evidence from the case.
  3. Create: Design a 12-week Munich accelerator programme that would help a student team find a 'Langfuse moment'. What three things would it offer that a university course cannot?
