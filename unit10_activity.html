BDIBM313 · Unit 10 · Ethics Tribunal

Ethics Tribunal — Who Pays for Safe AI?

Students hold a role-based hearing on outsourced AI data work, then write a fair outsourcing charter for their own venture.

Ethical Models and Practices Affecting Digitalization · Format: Ethics Tribunal

Learning Focus · ILOs 2, 3 & 4

Critically assess the technical and social conditions behind ethical problems in digital ventures, reflect on one's own ethical stance under pressure, and communicate how ethics shifts from traditional to digital business operations.

What a digital venture can do at scale and what it should do are separate questions — ask both before launch.

Warm-up · 10 min · Line of Conscience

The lecturer places a tape line across the room from 'Completely acceptable' to 'Completely unacceptable'. She reads three short statements, e.g. 'A start-up pays workers abroad the local minimum wage for traumatic data work', and students stand where they agree. After each statement two students from opposite ends explain their position in 30 seconds.

The Provocation

Somebody had to read the worst of the internet so that ChatGPT would not repeat it.

Legal in Nairobi, profitable in San Francisco, invisible in Munich. Is that ethical?

AI is never fully automatic. Somewhere, a human is in the loop — and on the payroll of someone else.

Being far away does not make a duty smaller.

Read the lines after showing the TIME headline of January 2023. Remind students that the tribunal judges arguments and responsibilities, not the guilt of individuals; all allegations must be stated as reported, with the companies' responses.

How the Activity Runs

1
Role briefing and evidence pack20 min

The class splits into six roles in groups of three to five: prosecution (argues that the client and contractor fell short of ethical duties), defence (argues the work was lawful, paid above local standards and necessary for AI safety), workers' representative, client (an AI company outsourcing labelling), regulator (applies GDPR, the EU AI Act and Kenyan context) and jury. Each group gets a one-page evidence pack with reported facts from TIME, the Guardian and TechCrunch, including Sama's and OpenAI's statements.

2
Prepare the case25 min

Groups prepare a three-minute opening statement on an A3 sheet, using at least two named frameworks (for example Three Ethical Theories, Carroll's CSR Pyramid, Fairwork Principles). The jury prepares its scoring sheet: strength of evidence, fairness to all parties, use of frameworks and realism of remedies.

3
The hearing40 min

The lecturer chairs. Openings (prosecution, defence, then workers' representative, client and regulator) take three minutes each, followed by one round of cross-examination where each role may ask one question to any other role. Speakers stand at a lectern; no slides and no laptops — only the A3 sheet.

4
Jury deliberation and verdict15 min

The jury deliberates in the open (the class listens but stays silent) and delivers a reasoned verdict on three questions: Were ethical duties met? Who carried which responsibility? What remedy is proportionate? The verdict must cite at least one framework by name.

5
Fair outsourcing charter15 min

Mixed groups (one member from each role) draft a five-point charter for outsourcing digital work in their own Digital Venture Project, scored against the five Fairwork principles. Charters are pinned on the wall and photographed.

Deliverable. Each mixed group submits a photographed five-point Fair Outsourcing Charter linked to the Fairwork principles; the jury submits its written verdict sheet.
