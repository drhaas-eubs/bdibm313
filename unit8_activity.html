BDIBM313 · Unit 8 · World Café

World Café — The Surplus Table

Students rotate between five hosted café tables, each exploring one sustainability question through the Too Good To Go case, and harvest a shared impact model.

Sustainable Entrepreneurship · Format: World Café

Learning Focus · ILOs 3 & 4

Evaluate how digital ventures build social and environmental value into their business models and support networks, and communicate how this differs from traditional, add-on approaches to sustainability.

Sustainable ventures win when the impact is built into the business model, not bolted on to the marketing.

Warm-up · 10 min · What's in Your Bin?

Each student writes on a sticky note one food item they threw away this week and why (forgot it, bought too much, 'best before' date passed). Notes go on a wall 'bin'. The lecturer clusters them live in three groups — planning, habits, labels — and asks: which of these could an app fix, and which could it never fix?

The Provocation

Roughly a third of the food produced in the world is lost or wasted.

Too Good To Go makes money every time a bakery fails to sell a croissant.

If the business model works perfectly, does the problem disappear — or the company?

A luddite refuses the tool. A greenwasher misuses it. Which one is more dangerous?

Impact you cannot find in the revenue model is probably only in the marketing.

Show each line on its own slide after the bin exercise. Pause on line three and invite two quick reactions: it sets up the café's central tension between profit, impact and mission.

How the Activity Runs

1
Café set-up and etiquette10 min

Arrange five tables with paper tablecloths, markers and one printed question each. Introduce the World Café etiquette (Brown and Isaacs, 2005): focus on what matters, listen together, connect ideas, draw on the tablecloth. One volunteer host per table stays for all rounds; phones are face down.

2
Round 1: Built-in or bolted-on?20 min

Groups of four to five sit at a table and discuss its question. Table 1: Where exactly is impact inside Too Good To Go's business model (Triple-Layered Business Model Canvas)? Table 2: Which digital challenges threaten it (data, algorithms, partner fraud, e-waste)? Table 3: Luddite or perceptive — how would a traditional baker react? Table 4: Who gives founders moral and professional support? Table 5: What must a sustainable founder keep learning? Everyone writes and draws on the tablecloth.

3
Round 2: Travel and cross-pollinate20 min

Everyone except the host moves to a new table, mixing with new people. The host summarises the previous conversation in two minutes; the newcomers challenge, add evidence from the case and link ideas from their first table. The host marks new ideas in a different colour.

4
Round 3: Design the next move20 min

Travellers move once more. Each table now turns its accumulated ideas into one concrete recommendation for Too Good To Go's next three years, using at least one named framework (e.g. Circular Economy Butterfly, Mentoring Functions, Technology Acceptance Model). The host writes the recommendation in one sentence at the centre of the tablecloth.

5
Harvest: Gallery walk and vote25 min

Tablecloths are hung on the wall. Each host pitches the table's recommendation in 90 seconds without slides. Students then place three dot stickers on the ideas most likely to create both impact and profit. The class agrees a final 'impact-built-in' checklist that the lecturer photographs for the portal.

Deliverable. Each table submits a photo of its tablecloth plus a one-sentence recommendation naming the framework used; the class produces a shared 'impact-built-in' checklist for use in the Digital Venture Project.
