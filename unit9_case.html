Builder.ai: When the Money Ran Out Faster Than the Story

A London AI unicorn that raised over $450 million entered insolvency in May 2025 after its numbers were questioned.

Builder.ai (Engineer.ai Corporation)United Kingdom2023–2025

Key Facts

  • In May 2023 Builder.ai announced a $250 million Series D led by the Qatar Investment Authority, bringing its total funding to over $450 million; it also announced an expanded partnership with Microsoft (TNGlobal, 2023).
  • Founder Sachin Dev Duggal stepped down as chief executive in early 2025 and Manpreet Ratia, a managing partner at investor Jungle Ventures, became CEO (Lawrence, 2025).
  • Bloomberg reported that the company had projected 2024 sales of about $220 million to lenders, while an independent audit put actual 2024 revenue at about $50 million (Sun, 2025b).
  • In April 2025 around 270 of roughly 770 staff were laid off; tech.eu reported that 2023 revenue had already been restated, which the company linked to Middle East resellers falling short of expectations (Lawrence, 2025).
  • Viola Credit, which led a $50 million loan facility provided in 2024, seized about $37 million from the company's accounts, leaving about $5 million; the CEO said no clear reason was given and Viola Credit did not comment (Sun, 2025a).
  • On 20 May 2025 the main unit, Engineer.ai Corporation, announced it would enter insolvency proceedings (Lawrence, 2025).
  • Bloomberg reported that the US Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York had subpoenaed the company for information on accounting policies and customer lists; the office declined to comment (Chakraborty, 2025).
  • Duggal did not respond to Bloomberg's requests for comment at the time; no court has made findings of wrongdoing in the sources cited (Sun, 2025b).

The Storyline (after Bostelaar)

Beginning · Misbelief

Investors and lenders believed that a famous founder, a big AI promise and strong backers such as Microsoft and a sovereign wealth fund were enough evidence. The more money arrived, the more the story seemed proven.

Middle · Insight

When an independent audit compared projections with real revenue, the gap was reported as roughly four times. Lenders with loan covenants acted first, cash disappeared within days, and the runway that looked like years collapsed to weeks.

End · Understanding

Fundraising is not proof of a business. Verified revenue, honest burn figures and a realistic runway are what keep a venture alive — and diligence must test them, however strong the narrative.

Your Task

In your team, reconstruct Builder.ai's financial position in early 2025 using the Burn Rate, Runway & Fume Date framework: what happens to runway when $37 million of $42 million disappears overnight? Then use How VCs Make Decisions to list which decision factors investors seem to have over-weighted and which evidence they should have demanded. Finally, apply Pecking Order Theory to explain why venture debt from Viola Credit made the company more fragile. Present your findings on one flip-chart page.

Discussion Questions

  1. Analyse: Which signals in the reported numbers (projected vs actual revenue, layoffs, the CEO change) should have alerted investors and lenders, and when?
  2. Evaluate: Was taking a $50 million loan facility a sound financing decision for a company with Builder.ai's reported revenue? Judge it with the Pecking Order and Burn Rate frameworks.
  3. Create: Design a one-page due-diligence checklist that a student VC panel could use to verify revenue claims of an AI start-up before investing.
