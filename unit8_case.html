Learning Focus · ILOs 3 & 4

Evaluate how digital ventures build social and environmental value into their business models and support networks, and communicate how this differs from traditional, add-on approaches to sustainability.

Too Good To Go: Profit From Every Unsold Croissant

How a Copenhagen app turned food waste into a B Corp business model — and reached profit while growing.

Too Good To GoDenmark (operating across Europe, North America and Asia-Pacific)2021–2026

Key Facts

  • In January 2021 Too Good To Go raised $31.1 million in its first institutional venture round, led by Blisce/; it then had 30 million registered users, 65,000 partner businesses, 15 countries and 50 million meals saved (Dillet, 2021).
  • The model connects consumers with shops that have surplus food: in the US the company takes $1.79 per 'Surprise Bag' sold and charges retailers an $89 annual membership fee (Wu and Duarte, 2024).
  • CNBC reported $162 million in revenue for the previous year and 100 million users across 19 countries in November 2024 (Wu and Duarte, 2024).
  • Too Good To Go ApS reported a profit of DKK 15.7 million for 2023 after a DKK 175.2 million loss in 2022, on revenue of DKK 545 million (Too Good To Go ApS, 2024).
  • The company is a Certified B Corporation and was recertified in early 2024 with a B Impact score of 93.4, up from 81.6 in its earlier assessment (Too Good To Go, n.d.a; B Lab, n.d.).
  • In 2026 Too Good To Go's website reported 120 million registered users, 180,000 partner businesses, 21 countries and more than 500 million meals saved since its founding in Copenhagen in 2016 (Too Good To Go, n.d.b).

The Storyline (after Bostelaar)

Beginning · Misbelief

Many people assume sustainability is a cost: something a company adds through donations, green packaging or a CSR report once it is profitable. Food waste looked like a charity problem, not a market.

Middle · Insight

Too Good To Go designed impact into the transaction itself. Every Surprise Bag sold saves food, earns the shop money it would otherwise lose, gives the customer a bargain and earns the platform a fee. Growth in revenue and growth in meals saved are the same number.

End · Understanding

When impact is built into the business model, scaling the business scales the impact, and certifications such as B Corp verify rather than decorate it. The remaining question is critical: a business that earns from surplus must still help partners to waste less in the first place.

Your Task

At your café table, map Too Good To Go on the Triple-Layered Business Model Canvas: fill the economic, environmental and social layers and draw arrows where one layer drives another. Then use Creating Shared Value to identify at which of the three levels (products and markets, value chain, local clusters) the company creates both social and economic value. Finally, apply the Circular Economy Butterfly to judge where food rescue sits in the hierarchy of keeping resources in use and propose one move that prevents surplus earlier in the chain.

Discussion Questions

  1. Analyse: Which elements of Too Good To Go's business model create economic, environmental and social value at the same time?
  2. Evaluate: Is a business that earns money from surplus food truly sustainable, or could it reduce the incentive for shops to prevent waste? Justify your view with evidence.
  3. Create: Design a new feature or service that would help partner shops produce less surplus while keeping Too Good To Go's revenue stable.
