Gorillas: Ten Minutes to Unicorn, Four Years to Shutdown

How a Berlin grocery app turned digital speed into a billion-dollar valuation, and why the physical side of the business caught up with it.

Gorillas Technologies GmbHGermany2020–2024

Key Facts

  • Gorillas was founded in Berlin in May 2020 and expanded aggressively during the coronavirus lockdowns; its founder and CEO was Kağan Sümer.
  • The app promised groceries at retail prices delivered in around ten minutes by riders on bikes from local micro-fulfilment centres (dark stores).
  • In March 2021 Gorillas raised a USD 290 million Series B led by Coatue, DST Global and Tencent, passing a USD 1 billion valuation in roughly ten months and becoming Europe’s fastest unicorn at the time; it then had more than 2,000 employees and over 40 micro-fulfilment centres.
  • In October 2021 a funding round of close to USD 1 billion valued Gorillas at USD 3.1 billion; Delivery Hero invested USD 235 million for a stake of about 8 per cent.
  • In May 2022 Gorillas cut around 300 jobs, mostly at its Berlin headquarters, and withdrew from Italy, Spain, Denmark and Belgium to focus on Germany, France, the Netherlands, the UK and the US, which generated about 90 per cent of revenue; it was reported to have been burning USD 50–75 million a month.
  • In December 2022 Turkish rival Getir acquired Gorillas in a deal that valued it at USD 1.2 billion, about 61 per cent below its 2021 valuation.
  • In April 2024 Getir announced its exit from Germany, the UK, the US and the rest of Europe to focus on Turkey; Gorillas was shut down, with around 1,100 jobs affected in Germany.

The Storyline (after Bostelaar)

Beginning · Misbelief

Gorillas and its investors believed that a beautiful app plus venture capital could make ten-minute delivery a mass habit everywhere at once. Digital speed in raising money, launching cities and winning downloads looked like proof that the model worked.

Middle · Insight

The app was digital, but most of the cost was physical: rent for dark stores, salaried riders, stock that spoils and bikes in the rain. Digital tools made it cheap to attract a customer, but every single order still needed a person to carry a bag up the stairs.

End · Understanding

Customers’ need for groceries did not change; what changed was who could serve them and how fast, but not the cost of the last kilometre. Digital entrepreneurship creates real advantages only where software replaces or radically cheapens the expensive part of the value chain.

Your Task

In groups of four, use Nambisan's Digital Entrepreneurship Elements to sort Gorillas’ business into digital artifacts, digital platform and digital infrastructure, and list what remained purely physical. Then place Gorillas on the Degrees of Digitization scale and justify your choice. Use Pipelines vs Platforms to explain why Gorillas behaved more like a linear pipeline with an app than like a true platform. Finish with one recommendation that would have changed the cost per order, presented in under two minutes.

Discussion Questions

  1. Analyse: Which parts of Gorillas’ value chain were genuinely digital, and which costs grew in direct proportion to every new order?
  2. Evaluate: Was the 2021 valuation of USD 3.1 billion a reasonable bet on digital transformation of grocery retail or a misjudgement of a hybrid business? Justify your answer with evidence from the facts.
  3. Create: Design a digitally enabled grocery concept for Munich students that keeps the customer benefit of speed but changes who does the physical work and at what cost.
