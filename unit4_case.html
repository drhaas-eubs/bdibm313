Vinted: From Burning Cash to Choosing Where to Spend

How a Lithuanian second-hand marketplace turned hard resource choices into Europe's first profitable pre-loved fashion platform.

VintedLithuania2021–2026

Key Facts

  • Vinted was founded in Lithuania in 2008; by 2015 it was 'burning money' and brought in Thomas Plantenga, who became CEO (Gollasch, 2025).
  • Plantenga cut headcount, centralised operations in Lithuania, closed overseas offices, removed seller fees and introduced a buyer protection fee instead (Macaulay, 2024).
  • Unusually for a digital-only brand, Vinted invested in TV advertising and in 2023 partnered with Channel 4 on a branded entertainment series to change perceptions of second-hand clothing (Gollasch, 2025).
  • In 2023 revenue grew 61% to €596.3 million and Vinted made its first net profit of €17.8 million, after a €20.4 million loss in 2022 (Vinted, 2024).
  • In 2023 Vinted also secured a €50 million revolving credit facility from BNP Paribas and ING, acquired the Dutch delivery company Homerr and installed about 1,500 Vinted Go lockers and pick-up points (Vinted, 2024).
  • In 2024 revenue rose 36% to €813.4 million and net profit to €76.7 million; a secondary share sale in October 2024 valued the company at €5 billion (Vinted, 2025).
  • In 2025 gross merchandise value grew 47% to €10.8 billion and revenue to €1.1 billion, while net profit fell 19% to €62 million as Vinted invested in Vinted Go, Vinted Pay and new markets (Vinted, 2026).

The Storyline (after Bostelaar)

Beginning · Misbelief

Many founders believe a marketplace should charge sellers, because they are the ones earning money, and that a digital company should only market itself online. Vinted followed this logic early on and was losing money.

Middle · Insight

Vinted removed seller fees to attract supply and charged buyers for protection instead, then spent on TV and on its own parcel network rather than only on online ads. Each choice meant saying no to something else, and profit arrived only in 2023.

End · Understanding

Opportunity in second-hand fashion was visible to everyone; Vinted won by deciding where to spend first: sellers, brand trust and logistics. In 2025 it again accepted lower profit to invest in delivery and payments, showing that strategy is a continuous resource decision.

Your Task

Your team is Vinted's strategy committee in early 2025. Use a SWOT/TOWS matrix to assess Vinted's options for growth, and the VRIO / Resource-Based View to test whether Vinted Go and Vinted Pay are real sources of competitive advantage. Then use the Bullseye Traction Framework and Paid–Owned–Earned Media (POEM) to propose how Vinted should split a €10 million marketing budget for entering a new market. Present your recommendation in a 3-minute board briefing.

Discussion Questions

  1. Analyse: Which resources and decisions (seller fees, buyer protection fee, TV, Vinted Go) best explain Vinted's move from loss to profit in 2023?
  2. Evaluate: In 2025 Vinted grew revenue but accepted lower profit to invest in logistics and payments. Was this the right use of scarce resources? Argue for or against.
  3. Create: Design a 12-month launch budget and cross-media channel plan for a Vinted-style marketplace for second-hand sports equipment in Germany, including a target CAC and LTV.
