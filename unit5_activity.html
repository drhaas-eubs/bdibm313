Case Study — The Cold-Call Clinic

Harvard-style cold-calling on the Langfuse case, then your team applies to a real Munich programme.

Business Incubators in Entrepreneurship in Digitalization · Format: Case Study

Learning Focus · ILOs 2 & 3

Critically assess how incubators, accelerators, investors and IP protection create the conditions for a digital idea to become a venture, and reflect on your own role when stakeholders put you on the spot.

Ecosystems do not create founders; they shorten the distance between an idea and the people who can make it real.

Warm-up · 10 min · Who Is in Your Corner?

On a sticky note, write the names (or roles) of three people who could help you start a digital business tomorrow: one for money, one for skills, one for customers. Stick them on the wall under three headings. Count how many notes are blank in each column and discuss in one minute: which resource is scarcest in this room?

The Provocation

Langfuse arrived at Y Combinator with a billing product. Three weeks later they threw it away.

The idea that made them successful did not exist when they applied.

Y Combinator did not give them the idea. It gave them 200 founders building AI products who all had the same problem.

So what are you really buying when you give an accelerator equity?

Read the lines slowly, then cold-call the first student immediately with the last question. Do not accept 'money' or 'mentoring' as an answer without asking 'and what else?'.

How the Activity Runs

1
Case briefing and rules of the clinic10 min

The lecturer sets the rules: anyone can be called, 'I don't know yet' is allowed once, and every answer must use one fact from the case. Students have the one-page case and the framework sheets open on the desk (no laptops).

2
Cold-call round 1: stakeholders and ecosystem30 min

The lecturer cold-calls individuals on who mattered to Langfuse and when. The class builds a live Power–Interest Grid and a Stakeholder Salience Venn on the whiteboard, placing YC partners, batch-mates, Lightspeed, open-source contributors, enterprise customers and ClickHouse. Students challenge each other's placements with evidence from the case.

3
Cold-call round 2: from idea to concept to IP30 min

Second round on the pivot: what job were developers hiring Langfuse to do, how did two-week idea tests resemble a Double Diamond, and why did an open-source (MIT) licence protect the business better than a patent would? Use Teece's Profiting from Innovation to discuss which complementary assets Langfuse built instead.

4
Group task: the incubator application35 min

Groups of 4 pick one real Munich programme (UnternehmerTUM Incubator or Accelerator, TUM Venture Labs, or WERK1) and draft a one-page application for their own Digital Venture Project idea on A3 paper: the opportunity, the customer job, the three stakeholders they most need, and what they would give and get. One member is the 'founder', one the 'sceptical programme manager'.

5
Selection board15 min

Each group has 2 minutes to defend its application while two students from another group act as the selection board and ask one hard question each. The board gives a 'yes', 'no' or 'come back with evidence' verdict with one reason.

Deliverable. An A3 programme application (photo uploaded to the course page) plus the class whiteboard stakeholder map, which feeds directly into the Digital Venture Project.
