BDIBM313 · Unit 6 · Prototype Sprint

Prototype Sprint — Paper Before Pixels

Build a paper or clickable prototype of your venture idea and test it live with real users from other groups.

Assessing Well-designed Prototypes and Building on Entrepreneurial Concepts · Format: Prototype Sprint

Learning Focus · ILOs 1 & 3

Investigate how digital prototyping and user testing improve traditional product development, and reflect on how you lead and respond when real users reject your design.

A prototype is a question you can hold in your hand — build it to learn, not to impress.

Warm-up · 10 min · The 30-Second Wallet

In pairs, sketch an app screen for your partner's wallet in 30 seconds without asking any questions. Then interview your partner for 2 minutes about how they actually pay, store cards and split bills, and redraw the screen. Compare the two sketches: what did the interview change?

The Provocation

Humane raised more than 230 million dollars and hired designers from Apple.

Its AI Pin was shown on a TED stage before most customers had touched one.

Within months, more pins were being returned than sold.

Not one feature failed because the engineers could not build it. It failed because nobody needed to wear it.

Today you will spend zero euros and learn more than Humane did before launch.

Show a photo of the AI Pin while reading. Pause after the fourth line and ask the room: what question should Humane have tested first, and how cheaply could they have tested it?

How the Activity Runs

1
Map the job and pick one question15 min

Groups of 4 take their Digital Venture Project idea and write one Jobs-to-be-Done statement and the single riskiest question (desirability, usability or willingness to pay). They choose what the prototype must prototype: role, look and feel, or implementation (Houde and Hill, 1997).

2
Sketch and storyboard15 min

Each member silently sketches a solution on A4 (Crazy 8s style), then the group votes with dots and builds a 5–6 frame storyboard of the user's journey from first contact to completed task.

3
Build the prototype30 min

Build a paper prototype (A4 screens, sticky-note buttons) or a quick clickable mock-up in Figma or Canva. Add real content: a headline, onboarding text, a price and one call to action. Prepare 3 test tasks and a short Kano question sheet with 4 features.

4
Live user testing30 min

Groups swap testers: two members visit other groups as test users while two stay as facilitator and note-taker. Each test lasts about 7 minutes: think-aloud on the 3 tasks, then the Kano questions. Note-takers log every usability problem against Nielsen's heuristics.

5
Iterate and plan the launch20 min

Groups cluster findings on a wall, decide 'iterate, pivot or persevere', and redraw the two worst screens. They then sketch a one-line time-to-market plan: who are the first 100 early adopters and which AARRR metric will they track first?

Deliverable. Photos of the original and iterated prototype, a one-page test log (tasks, problems found, Kano results) and a launch-plan line naming the first early-adopter segment and its key metric.
